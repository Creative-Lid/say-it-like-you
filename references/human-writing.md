# Human writing review

Use this reference for a dedicated humanizing or editing pass.

## Information before shape

Every kept sentence should contribute a fact, consequence, decision, useful reaction, or deliberate piece of voice. Cut sentences that only announce, repeat, or inflate what surrounds them.

Preserve the source's claims even when changing its structure. Watch qualifiers, rankings, causal claims, dates, and words such as `all`, `only`, `always`, and `never`. A smoother sentence is not an excuse to make a stronger claim.

## Structural patterns

### Empty contrast

`Not X but Y` is useful when X is a real belief being corrected. It is padding when X exists only to make Y sound important.

### Dramatic closer

A short final sentence works when it adds a consequence or lands a genuine line. Cut it when it merely tells the reader that the previous paragraph mattered.

### Invented opposition

Do not create an unnamed skeptic so the writer can defeat them. Keep objections that the audience would genuinely consider or that the source attributes to someone.

### Forced completeness

Three parallel items are not automatically suspicious. Keep three real parts. Merge, develop, or vary them when the list exists only to sound complete.

### Inflated importance

Remove claims that an ordinary update is pivotal, transformative, groundbreaking, or a testament to something unless the source supports that significance.

### Borrowed authority

Name who said what when the source provides it. Do not use `experts say` or a list of prestigious outlets as decoration.

### Mechanical formatting

Use headings, bold, and lists when they help readers navigate. Do not give every bullet a bold label, place an emoji before every section, or repeat a heading in the sentence below it.

### Chat residue

Standalone copy should not contain assistant greetings, praise for the prompt, offers to continue, or explanations of how the draft was assembled.

## Preserve real voice

Keep specific odd details, honest uncertainty, uneven emphasis, first-person choices, era-bound references, and self-corrections when they fit the writer. Human writing is not merely clean writing with shorter sentences.
